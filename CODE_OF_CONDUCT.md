# Community Code of Conduct

## Our standard

Project spaces must remain safe, respectful, and useful for technical collaboration.
Participants are expected to:

- discuss ideas and behavior rather than attacking people;
- welcome different experience levels, backgrounds, and viewpoints;
- give specific, actionable feedback;
- respect privacy, consent, attribution, and confidentiality;
- avoid sexualized language, threats, harassment, stalking, doxxing, and sustained
  disruption; and
- stop an unwelcome interaction when asked.

These expectations apply to issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project-run
events, and private communication used to conduct project business.

## Enforcement

Authorized conduct moderators may edit or remove content, pause an interaction,
restrict participation, or ban a participant when necessary to protect the community.
Responses should be proportionate, documented privately, and avoid unnecessary
disclosure about affected people.

No conduct moderator or confidential project conduct-reporting route is assigned in
this snapshot. That governance gap must be resolved before the project claims an open
community. Do not disclose sensitive conduct reports in a public issue. For conduct
that violates GitHub's platform policies, use GitHub's own abuse-reporting controls.

Once the project publishes a tested private conduct route, this file must identify it
without requiring reporters to expose personal information publicly. A moderator with
a conflict of interest must recuse from the report.

## Scope and appeals

Project moderators may act on conduct outside project spaces when it directly affects
participant safety or the project's ability to collaborate. A sanctioned participant
may request review by an uninvolved moderator after at least two moderators are
assigned. Until then, the project cannot promise an internal appeal panel.

Retaliation against a reporter, witness, or moderator acting in good faith is itself a
violation.
